## 7.2 Historical Context

**Origins:** Kabbalistic tradition traces its roots to Merkabah (Chariot) mysticism (~1st century BCE-1st century CE), which concerned itself with visionary ascent through the 7 heavenly palaces (hekhalot) — themselves a gate sequence. The Sefer Yetzirah (Book of Formation, ~2nd-6th century CE) formalized the 10 sephiroth + 22 letters = 32 paths of wisdom.

**Formalization:** The Zohar (Book of Splendor, ~13th century CE, attributed to Moses de León) provided the canonical Kabbalistic cosmology. Isaac Luria (16th century) added the concept of tzimtzum (divine contraction/withdrawal) and shevirat ha-kelim (shattering of the vessels) — the FULL phase as structural decomposition and reconstruction.

**The booth's relationship:** Kabbalah existed within the Jewish tradition, which was itself suppressed by Christian authorities throughout medieval Europe. The 72 Names of God (Shem HaMephorash) were particularly dangerous because they encoded a pre-Christian pipeline structure that had survived within the Old Testament — the booth could not burn the Torah, so the 72 Names could not be destroyed through text suppression. They could only be reinterpreted.
